# Terra Active — KPI Framework

## Objective

This document defines the key performance indicators (KPIs) used to evaluate Terra Active's customer, commercial, digital, community, marketing and operational performance.

KPIs are linked to the business questions defined in `02_Business_Questions.md`.

Where relevant, metrics should be analysed across customer segments, product categories, geographic markets, acquisition channels and time periods.

---

## 1. Customer & Retention KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Purpose | Caveats / Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) | Cumulative gross profit generated by a customer over the observed relationship with Terra Active | Identify Terra Active's most economically valuable customers and customer segments | Historical CLV favours customers with longer observed histories. Comparisons should therefore account for customer tenure |
| Repeat Purchase Rate | Customers with at least two completed orders / customers with at least one completed order | Measure Terra Active's ability to generate repeat purchasing | Does not capture differences in purchase frequency beyond the second order and is sensitive to the observation window |
| Purchase Frequency | Number of completed orders per customer over a defined period | Compare purchasing behaviour across customer segments | Must be calculated over comparable observation periods; otherwise older customers mechanically have more opportunities to purchase |
| Average Order Value (AOV) | Net revenue / number of completed orders | Measure average basket value and monitor cross-selling opportunities | A higher AOV is not necessarily more profitable if driven by discounts, low-margin products or high return rates |
| Time to Second Purchase | Number of days between a customer's first and second completed order | Understand the early customer lifecycle and identify opportunities to encourage repeat purchasing | Only observable for customers who make a second purchase; customers acquired recently may not have had sufficient time to repurchase |
| Customer Retention Rate | Percentage of customers remaining active according to a defined purchase or engagement criterion over a specified period | Measure Terra Active's ability to maintain customer relationships | "Active" must be explicitly defined. Appropriate retention windows may differ across customer segments and purchasing patterns |

## 2. Product & Commercial KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Purpose | Caveats / Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross Sales | Total value of products sold before discounts and refunds | Measure top-line product demand before commercial adjustments | Can overstate realised commercial performance when discounting or returns are significant |
| Net Revenue | Gross sales − discounts − refunds | Measure realised sales after major commercial adjustments | Revenue alone does not capture product costs or overall profitability |
| Gross Profit | Net revenue − cost of goods sold (COGS) | Compare the economic contribution of products and categories | Does not include all operating expenses such as marketing, fulfilment or corporate overhead |
| Gross Margin % | Gross profit / net revenue | Compare profitability across products and categories of different sizes | High-margin products may still contribute little total profit if sales volume is low |
| Accessory Attachment Rate | Apparel orders containing at least one accessory / total apparel orders | Measure Terra Active's success at cross-selling accessories | Changes may reflect shifts in product or customer mix rather than improved cross-selling alone |
| Units per Transaction (UPT) | Total units sold / number of completed orders | Measure basket size in terms of number of products | Treats low-value and high-value products equally and should therefore be interpreted alongside AOV and margin |
| Unit Return Rate | Returned units / units sold | Identify products or categories with unusually high return behaviour | Return rates may vary structurally by category, size, country or customer segment |
| Return Value Rate | Refunded product value / gross sales | Measure the financial impact of product returns | High-value products have greater influence on this metric than low-value products |
| Sell-Through Rate | Units sold / units available for sale over a defined period | Evaluate how effectively inventory converts into sales | Strong sell-through can reflect insufficient inventory rather than optimal merchandising |

## 3. Digital Product KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Purpose | Caveats / Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product View → Cart Rate | Sessions containing an add-to-cart event / sessions containing a product-view event | Identify how effectively product pages generate purchase intent | Differences may reflect product mix, traffic quality or customer intent rather than page experience alone |
| Cart → Checkout Rate | Sessions beginning checkout / sessions containing an add-to-cart event | Identify friction between product selection and checkout | Customers may intentionally use carts as wishlists or return in later sessions |
| Checkout → Purchase Rate | Sessions containing a purchase / sessions beginning checkout | Identify checkout-stage friction | Payment failures, shipping costs and technical issues may all influence the metric and require deeper investigation |
| Purchase Conversion Rate | Sessions resulting in a purchase / eligible sessions | Measure the overall effectiveness of the digital commerce experience | Highly sensitive to traffic source, customer intent and the definition of an eligible session |
| Monthly Active Users (MAU) | Unique users performing at least one qualifying app activity during a calendar month | Measure the size of Terra Active's actively engaged digital audience | Qualifying activity must be defined; simply opening the app may not represent meaningful engagement |
| Feature Adoption Rate | Users interacting with a feature / eligible active users | Measure adoption of features such as favourites, challenges or recommendations | Low adoption can result from poor discoverability rather than low customer value |
| Recommendation Conversion Rate | Purchases following interaction with a recommendation / recommendation interactions | Evaluate the commercial performance of personalised recommendations | Observational conversion does not establish incremental impact; an A/B test is preferable for causal evaluation |

## 4. Community & Event KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Purpose | Caveats / Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event Registration Rate | Event registrations / eligible event-page viewers | Measure customer interest in Terra Active events | Requires reliable event-page exposure data and may be affected by event capacity |
| Event Attendance Rate | Attendees / registered participants | Measure how effectively registrations translate into actual participation | Weather, location, event price and cancellation policies can materially influence attendance |
| Event Capacity Utilisation | Registrations or attendees / event capacity | Identify events experiencing excess or insufficient demand | A sold-out event does not reveal how much additional latent demand existed |
| Repeat Event Participation Rate | Customers attending at least two events / customers attending at least one event | Measure recurring community engagement | Customers with longer membership histories have more opportunities to attend multiple events |
| Post-Event Purchase Rate | Event attendees making a purchase within a defined period after attendance / total attendees | Examine the relationship between community participation and subsequent purchasing | Higher post-event purchasing does not establish that the event caused the increase; attendees may already be more engaged customers |
| Active Club Member Rate | Club members completing at least one qualifying Club activity during a defined period / total Club members | Measure meaningful participation rather than membership alone | The definition of qualifying activity materially affects the metric |
| Challenge Completion Rate | Members completing a challenge / members who joined the challenge | Measure engagement with Terra Active challenges | Challenge difficulty and duration should be considered when comparing completion rates |

## 5. Marketing & Acquisition KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Purpose | Caveats / Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) | Marketing spend / new customers acquired | Measure the cost of acquiring new customers | Attribution methodology determines which customers and costs are assigned to each channel |
| Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) | Revenue attributed to advertising / advertising spend | Measure short-term revenue generated relative to advertising investment | Revenue-based ROAS does not account for margin, returns or long-term customer value |
| CLV:CAC Ratio | Customer lifetime value / customer acquisition cost | Compare long-term customer value with acquisition cost | Historical CLV may be incomplete for newer cohorts, while CAC depends heavily on attribution methodology |
| Campaign Conversion Rate | Customers completing the target action / customers exposed to or interacting with a campaign | Evaluate campaign effectiveness | The denominator must be clearly defined; exposure-based and click-based conversion answer different questions |
| New Customer Rate | New customers acquired / total purchasing customers during a period | Monitor Terra Active's ability to expand its customer base | Strong customer acquisition does not necessarily imply high-quality or profitable growth |
| Discount Dependency | Share of customer purchases made using discounts or promotions | Identify customers and segments whose purchasing behaviour depends heavily on promotions | Frequent promotional exposure may itself create discount dependency, making interpretation more complex |

## 6. Inventory, Weather & Seasonality KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Purpose | Caveats / Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stockout Rate | Product-location-days out of stock / total product-location-days | Measure product availability and identify inventory shortages | A stockout does not directly reveal lost demand because some customers may substitute another product or postpone their purchase |
| Estimated Lost Sales from Stockouts | Estimated expected demand during stockout periods − observed fulfilled demand | Quantify the potential commercial impact of inventory shortages | Lost sales must be estimated because unobserved demand cannot be measured directly |
| Inventory Turnover | COGS / average inventory value | Measure how efficiently inventory is being sold and replenished | Appropriate turnover differs by product category and season |
| Sell-Through Rate | Units sold / units available for sale during a defined period | Measure product demand relative to available inventory | Very high sell-through may indicate successful demand or insufficient initial inventory |
| Weather Sensitivity | Change in product demand associated with changes in defined weather variables | Identify categories whose demand is particularly weather-dependent | Weather relationships may be confounded by seasonality, promotions, holidays and geographic differences |
| Forecast MAE | Mean absolute difference between forecast and actual demand | Evaluate demand forecast accuracy in original units | MAE is scale-dependent, making comparisons across products with very different sales volumes difficult |
| Forecast WAPE | Sum of absolute forecast errors / sum of actual demand | Compare aggregate forecast accuracy across periods or product groups | Can behave poorly when actual demand is extremely low and should not be interpreted without volume context |

## 7. KPI Interpretation Principles

Terra Active's KPI framework follows several analytical principles:

1. **Metrics should be interpreted in context.**  
   A KPI should rarely be evaluated in isolation. For example, increasing AOV is not necessarily positive if return rates increase or gross margin declines.

2. **Segmentation matters.**  
   Aggregate metrics may hide meaningful differences across customer segments, countries, product categories, acquisition channels and devices.

3. **Observation windows must be consistent.**  
   Customer metrics such as CLV, purchase frequency and repeat purchasing should account for differences in customer tenure.

4. **Correlation does not imply causation.**  
   Observational relationships — particularly around Club membership, event participation, app engagement and purchasing — should not automatically be interpreted as causal effects.

5. **Metric definitions must remain consistent.**  
   Terms such as "active customer", "retained customer", "eligible session" and "qualifying activity" must be explicitly defined before analysis.

6. **Commercial performance should consider both growth and economics.**  
   Revenue, conversion and customer acquisition should be evaluated alongside profitability, returns and long-term customer value.
